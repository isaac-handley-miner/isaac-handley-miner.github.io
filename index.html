Isaac J. Handley-Miner, Ph.D.

Postdoctoral Research Fellow, Boston College
Director, Transparent Replications Project

isaac.handley-miner@bc.edu

I am a postdoc at Boston College and the incoming director of the Transparent Replications Project.

In my postdoc, I study how people think about truth and credibility. My research draws on social psychology and cognitive science to investigate what leads people and AI models to consider information true or false.

I am also an active contributor to open science and metascience efforts.

At the Transparent Replication Project, I direct initiatives designed to celebrate high quality psychological research and shift the field's incentives toward more replicable, reliable methods.

Additionally, I serve as a BITSS Catalyst at the Berkeley Initiative for Transparency in the Social Sciences, a member of the Pilot Reporting Task Force, and a member of the REPEAT Network at Psychological Science, where I conduct computational reproducibility checks for papers before they are published.

I have a B.A. in economics from Hamilton College and a Ph.D. in psychology from Boston College.
